The setter call.

You are not selling a roof. You are booking a free inspection on a house the National Weather Service already told us got hit.

The whole edge in one line: every name on your list is a house inside a confirmed hail footprint. You know the date. You know the stone size. They usually don't. That is the only reason this call works, so lead with it.

Before you dial

  • Pull the record: storm date, hail size, city. Have it on screen.
  • Check the DNC flag in the tool. Flagged red, you do not dial. No exceptions.
  • Calling window: 8am–8pm local. Texas and federal rules both.

The open (first 10 seconds)

You: "Hi, is this {{NAME}}? … {{NAME}}, this is {{REP}} with PowerEdge here in {{CITY}}. I'm calling about the hail that came through your area on {{DATE}}. The weather service logged {{SIZE}}-inch stones right around your street. Did you ever get your roof looked at after that one?"

Stop talking. The question does the work. Most people answer honestly because you gave them a real date instead of "storms in the area."

Where it goes from there

"No, nobody's looked at it."

You: "That's most people. Hail damage doesn't leak right away. It bruises the shingle and the roof fails a couple years later, usually after the claim window has closed. We're out in {{CITY}} this week doing free inspections. We get up there, photograph everything, and you get it in writing either way. If your roof is fine, I'd rather tell you that than sell you something. Want me to put you down for {{DAY}} morning or {{DAY}} afternoon?"

"Somebody already looked at it."

You: "Good. Who'd you have out? … And did they give you anything in writing, photos and measurements? … That's the part that matters. A lot of guys eyeball it from the driveway. If you've got a written report you're set, and I'll leave you alone. If you don't, it costs you nothing to have ours."

"I already filed / already got a roof."

You: "Then you're ahead of everybody. Did they do the whole roof or just a repair? … Any issue with the work? … Alright, I'll make a note so we stop calling you. One thing. If anybody knocks claiming they can get your deductible waived, that's a crime in Texas and it'll come back on you, not them. Just so you know."

That last line costs nothing and it is the reason people remember us. Say it.

Objections

They sayYou say
"I'm not interested." "Fair enough. Can I ask, is that because you've already had it checked, or because you're tired of roofers calling?" (Both answers give you somewhere to go. The second one, agree with them: "I'd be tired of it too. That's why we send the report whether we get the job or not.")
"How much does it cost?" "Inspection is free, and that's not a hook. There's nothing to buy at the inspection. You get photos and a written assessment, and then you decide what you want to do."
"My roof's fine." "It might be. Hail damage you can see from the ground is already a bad roof. What we're looking for is bruising you can only find up top. Twenty minutes and you'll know for sure."
"My insurance will drop me if I file." "That's a real concern and I'm not going to tell you it can't happen. What I'd say is: an inspection isn't a claim. Find out what you've got first, then decide whether filing makes sense. Plenty of people get our report and sit on it."
"I'll call you back." "Sure. One thing worth knowing. Most Texas policies put a deadline on filing from the date of the storm, and yours was {{DATE}}. Not trying to rush you, just don't want you to find out in two years. Want me to hold {{DAY}} and you call me if it doesn't work?"
"Are you going door to door?" "No, I'm calling from our office in DFW. We pulled the weather service hail reports for {{CITY}} and your street was inside the footprint on {{DATE}}."
"How'd you get my number?" "Public property records plus the storm map. Nothing fancy, and if you'd rather not hear from us I'll take you off right now."

Locking the appointment

  • Offer two windows, never "when are you free." "{{DAY}} 8–10, or {{DAY}} 1–3?"
  • Confirm the address out loud and spell the street back.
  • Get an email. "I'll send you the confirmation so you've got our name and license info in writing."
  • Tell them what happens: "{{CLOSER}} will call you before he heads over. He'll be in a PowerEdge truck. Takes about 30–45 minutes and you don't have to be on the roof with him."
  • Book it in the tool before the next dial. An appointment in your notebook is not an appointment.
Never say any of this: that we'll waive, cover, eat, or "work with" the deductible. That we can get the claim approved. That we handle or negotiate the claim. That we're licensed by the state for roofing. Texas doesn't license roofers. Any of those can end the company, not just the call. See the Texas Compliance Card.
