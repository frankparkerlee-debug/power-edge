Rev. Nov 2026

The door. Ten seconds, one question, one booking.

You are not selling a roof. You are booking a free inspection on a house the National Weather Service says got hit. The app shows you the date and the stone size for that street. Lead with them.

Before you knock

  • Your permit for this city is in your pocket. No permit, no knocking in that city.
  • Open the Map tab and tap Near me. Knock the houses with a dark ring first: those are priority.
  • Tap the house, then Roof and storms. Know its hail date and stone size before you walk up.
  • No Soliciting sign: walk past. Don't knock, don't leave anything.
  • Knock only inside the city's hours. Your Getting started checklist lists them for each city.

The open (first ten seconds)

You: "Hi, I'm {{YOUR NAME}} with PowerEdge. I'm not selling anything today. The weather service logged {{SIZE}}-inch hail on this street on {{DATE}}, and we're checking roofs on this block this week. Has anybody been up on yours since then?"

Then stop talking. A real date and a real stone size is why this works. "Storms in the area" is what every other knocker says.

Booking at the door

  1. Tap Book inspection.
  2. Pick a window with them. Only windows a closer is free for show up, so whatever they pick is real.
  3. Confirm the name of the person you spoke to and their phone or email. They get a confirmation.
  4. Leave the closer a note: what they told you (a leak, the roof's age, an earlier inspection, who decides).
  5. Hand them an appointment card with the day and window written on it.
Book times they'll keep. You're paid $50 when the inspection is held: the homeowner is home and the closer finishes it. A booking nobody shows up for pays nothing and costs the closer an hour.

The app's record of who booked each inspection decides who gets paid on that roof. A door you don't log is a door nobody can credit to you.

Never at the door: offer to cover, waive or "work with" a deductible · say insurance will buy them a new roof · say you'll file or handle their claim · call PowerEdge a licensed roofer · knock in a city where you don't hold the permit · sign anyone up for texts (they use the Storm alerts sign-up themselves, or not at all) · push past a no.

What good looks like
