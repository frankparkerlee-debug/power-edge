How a roof claim actually works.

Written by a roofing company, about the things roofing companies usually don’t tell you. Keep it. It’s useful even if you never hire us.

The five steps

  1. Someone gets on the roof. Hail damage is bruising, and you generally cannot see it from the driveway. If a contractor quotes you without going up, that is a sales call, not an inspection.
  2. You decide whether to file. Not the contractor. An inspection is not a claim, and plenty of people take the report and sit on it.
  3. You file with your carrier. In Texas, a roofing contractor is not allowed to file or negotiate the claim for you. Anyone who offers is breaking the law.
  4. The adjuster comes out. Your contractor can be there with photos and measurements so nothing legitimate gets missed. The carrier sets the scope and the price.
  5. The work gets done, and you pay your deductible. That part is not optional.

Four things worth knowing

Your deductible is yours to pay. Texas Business & Commerce Code § 27.02 makes it an offense for a contractor to waive, rebate, or absorb it, and for you to go along with it. If someone offers, two things are true: they are willing to break the law, and they are padding the estimate somewhere to make the money back.
Your contractor is not your adjuster. Texas Insurance Code § 4102.163 bars a contractor from adjusting a claim on a property where they want the work. We can document, estimate, and talk to your adjuster about what we found. We cannot tell you what your policy covers.
Texas does not license roofers. There is no state roofing license, so “licensed roofer” means nothing here. Ask instead for a certificate of liability insurance, a workmanship warranty in writing, and a local address that is not a PO box.
Claims have deadlines. Most Texas policies require you to file within a set period after the date of loss. If hail hit your home last spring, that window is closing while the roof looks fine from the ground.
If the deductible is the problem, ask who is lending. Some roofers hand you to a finance company; we lend in house. Either way the deductible gets paid in full, because a contractor covering it is committing a crime. Anyone offering to make it disappear is telling you what kind of work you are about to get.

Questions to ask any roofer at your door

  • Can I see your certificate of liability insurance right now?
  • Are you getting on the roof, and will I get photos and measurements in writing?
  • What happens if my claim is denied, and what do I owe you?
  • Who does the work: your own crews, or subcontractors?
  • What does your workmanship warranty cover, and for how long?
  • Will you be reachable in two years if something leaks?
